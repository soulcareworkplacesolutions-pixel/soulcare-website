The 3 PM Slump at Work: Why Energy Crashes After Lunch

SoulCare™ Specialist Team · 25 November 2026

Walk through most offices at 3 PM and you can see it. Screens stay open, but replies slow down, meetings drift and the same paragraph gets read twice. Most people assume they simply need more coffee. In practice, the afternoon slump is a predictable mix of body clock, lunch, posture and the morning's workload, and most of it can be managed.

Why energy drops in the afternoon

  • The body clock. Alertness naturally dips for many people in the early afternoon. It is normal, but the workday rarely plans for it.
  • A heavy lunch. Large, refined-carbohydrate meals tend to leave people sluggish an hour or two later.
  • Hours of sitting. Sitting still for long stretches reduces how alert people feel, and stiff necks and backs add their own drag.
  • Too little water. Many people drink far less at their desks than they think.
  • A morning of decisions. By mid-afternoon your most responsible people have already made hundreds of choices. We explain this in Decision Fatigue at Work.
  • Short sleep. A late night shows up most clearly after lunch.

What it costs a team

The slump rarely shows up as a sick day. It shows up as slower work, more small errors, shorter tempers in late meetings and important tasks pushed to tomorrow. For a small team, a lost hour each afternoon across ten or twenty people adds up quickly.

Six changes that help

  1. Move demanding work to the morning. Keep planning, reviews and difficult calls before lunch, and leave routine tasks for the afternoon.
  2. Lighten lunch. A plate with protein, vegetables and fibre keeps energy steadier than a large rice or bread-heavy meal.
  3. Walk for ten minutes after eating. A short walk after lunch is one of the simplest ways to lift afternoon alertness.
  4. Build in movement breaks. Stand up, stretch the neck and shoulders, or take a call on your feet at least once an hour.
  5. Keep water at the desk. Make refilling a bottle part of the routine, not an afterthought.
  6. Protect sleep. Late-night messages and calls carry over into the next afternoon. Agree as a team when messages can wait until morning.
A quick check for managers: note when your team's most important meetings happen this week. If most fall between 2 PM and 4 PM, moving even one of them to the morning is an easy first change.

How to know if it is working
