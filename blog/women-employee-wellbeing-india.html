Women's Wellbeing at Work in India: What Employers Should Know in 2026

SoulCare™ Specialist Team · 25 November 2026

Women in India's offices are carrying more strain than their male colleagues, and the data from 2026 makes that hard to ignore. A study of corporate employees by cult.fit found that 72% of women reported high levels of tension, compared with 54% of men. For employers, women's wellbeing at work is no longer a side topic for Women's Day. It affects who stays, who rises into management and how well teams perform.

Where women drop out of the pipeline

Great Place To Work India's Women at Work 2026 report, based on 4.05 lakh employee responses across 375 organisations, shows the pattern clearly. Women make up 32% of individual contributors, but only 20% of frontline managers and 18% of middle management.

The report calls the years between these levels a "collision zone": the years when people build leadership experience are often the same years as marriage, pregnancy, childcare and caring for parents. Women also reported fewer chances to grow. 46% said they had meaningful opportunities to develop new ways of working, nine points lower than men.

Menstrual health and the leave policy debate

Menstrual health has become the most discussed women's workplace issue in India this year. In Naukri's survey of over 70,000 women professionals for Women's Day 2026, menstrual leave was the top policy request, chosen by 34%, and 75% said their workplace does not support it.

The legal position is still developing:

  • No national law. On 13 March 2026, the Supreme Court declined to direct mandatory paid menstrual leave for all working women, warning that a compulsory rule could discourage employers from hiring women. It asked the Ministry of Women and Child Development to consider a model policy.
  • Karnataka. The Menstrual Leave Policy 2025 gives women aged 18 to 52 one paid day of menstrual leave a month, up to 12 days a year, including in the private sector. On 15 April 2026, the Karnataka High Court upheld the policy and directed strict implementation.
  • Other states. Bihar has given state government employees two paid days a month since 1992, and Odisha introduced one paid day a month for women state government employees in October 2024.

This is a summary for awareness, not legal advice. Check the rules that apply in your state and sector.

The health issues that rarely reach HR

Many of the health concerns women live with at work, such as painful periods, PCOS, thyroid problems, anaemia, migraines and the changes of perimenopause, seldom need a hospital stay. That is exactly why they go unnoticed. They show up instead as quiet fatigue, lost focus and days pushed through at half capacity, while standard benefits and policies are built around hospital visits.

Six practical steps for employers

  1. Offer flexibility where the work allows it. In Naukri's survey, 1 in 2 women said hybrid or remote options would most improve their work-life balance. Flexible start times or one work-from-home day can matter more than a new benefit.
  2. Write a simple, private menstrual health policy. Whether it is a leave day, a work-from-home option or flexible hours, keep it easy to use, based on self-declaration and free of awkward questions.
  3. Get the basics right in the office. Clean, well-stocked washrooms, a quiet space to rest and sanitary products available without asking.
  4. Make health checks relevant. Include screening that reflects women's health, such as haemoglobin and thyroid checks, and give people time off to attend them.
  5. Train managers to notice and to ask. Most strain is invisible until a manager asks a simple, private question and listens without judgement.
  6. Measure it, anonymously. Ask your team how they are doing on pain, energy, sleep and stress, without names attached, then act on what the answers show and ask again.
A quick check for leaders: look at how many women were promoted into their first manager role in your organisation in the last year, compared with how many were eligible. That one number often shows where support is missing.
