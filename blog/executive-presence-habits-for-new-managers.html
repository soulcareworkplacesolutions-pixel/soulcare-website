Executive Presence: Five Habits New Managers Can Build

SoulCare™ Specialist Team · 12 November 2026

Many new managers believe executive presence is something people are born with: a confident voice, a commanding personality, the right look. In practice, leadership presence is built from a small set of habits that anyone can practise. Teams notice these habits long before they notice job titles.

Five habits that build executive presence

  1. Arrive prepared, not perfect. Know the purpose of every meeting you lead and the one decision you need from it. Preparation reads as confidence.
  2. Practise active listening. Let people finish. Summarise what you heard before you respond. Few habits build trust faster.
  3. Speak in shorter sentences. Clear, simple language signals clear thinking. Lead with the point, then give the reason.
  4. Stay steady under pressure. When something goes wrong, the team watches the manager's face first. A calm pause before responding is a leadership skill.
  5. Carry yourself with intention. Posture, eye contact and how you enter a room all communicate before you speak. Small adjustments make a visible difference.

Confidence at work comes from practice

Self-doubt is common in new managers, especially those promoted from within the team. The answer is rarely to "feel more confident" first. It is to practise the behaviours above until they become natural. Confidence follows action, not the other way round.

Try this for one week: before every meeting you lead, write one line: "The decision we need today is..." Then open the meeting with that line.

Why companies invest in presence

Managers shape the daily experience of almost every employee. Helping them communicate clearly, listen well and stay steady under pressure improves team trust, decision quality and retention, often more than any single policy change.
